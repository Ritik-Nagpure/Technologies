## PART 3 – MOTION

**Q46. What is motion?**

**Answer:** A change in the position of an object with time relative to a reference point.

**Q47. What is rest?**

**Answer:** The condition in which an object's position does not change with time relative to a reference point.

**Q48. What is distance?**

**Answer:** The total length of the actual path travelled by an object.

**Q49. What is displacement?**

**Answer:** The shortest straight-line distance from the initial position to the final position, with direction.

**Q50. Is distance a scalar or vector quantity?**

**Answer:** Scalar quantity.

**Q51. Is displacement a scalar or vector quantity?**

**Answer:** Vector quantity.

**Q52. What is speed?**

**Answer:** Distance travelled per unit time.

**Q53. What is the formula for speed?**

**Answer:** Speed = Distance / Time.

**Q54. What is velocity?**

**Answer:** Displacement per unit time in a specified direction.

**Q55. What is the formula for average speed?**

**Answer:** Total distance / Total time.

**Q56. What is the formula for average velocity?**

**Answer:** Total displacement / Total time.

**Q57. What is acceleration?**

**Answer:** The rate of change of velocity with time.

**Q58. What is the formula for acceleration?**

**Answer:** Acceleration = (Final velocity − Initial velocity) / Time.

**Q59. What is the SI unit of acceleration?**

**Answer:** m/s².

**Q60. What is uniform motion?**

**Answer:** Motion in which an object covers equal distances in equal intervals of time.

**Q61. What is non-uniform motion?**

**Answer:** Motion in which an object covers unequal distances in equal intervals of time.

**Q62. What is uniform acceleration?**

**Answer:** Equal changes in velocity in equal intervals of time.

**Q63. What is retardation?**

**Answer:** Negative acceleration or acceleration opposite to the direction of velocity.

**Q64. What is the first equation of motion?**

**Answer:** v = u + at.

**Q65. What is the second equation of motion?**

**Answer:** s = ut + ½at².

**Q66. What is the third equation of motion?**

**Answer:** v² = u² + 2as.

**Q67. What do u, v, a, s and t represent in equations of motion?**

**Answer:** u = initial velocity, v = final velocity, a = acceleration, s = displacement, t = time.

**Q68. What is the acceleration of an object moving with constant velocity?**

**Answer:** Zero.

**Q69. What is the shape of a distance-time graph for uniform speed?**

**Answer:** A straight line.

**Q70. What does the slope of a distance-time graph represent?**

**Answer:** Speed.

**Q71. What does the slope of a velocity-time graph represent?**

**Answer:** Acceleration.

**Q72. What does the area under a velocity-time graph represent?**

**Answer:** Displacement.

**Q73. What is circular motion?**

**Answer:** Motion of an object along a circular path.

**Q74. What is uniform circular motion?**

**Answer:** Motion along a circular path at constant speed.

**Q75. Is uniform circular motion accelerated motion?**

**Answer:** Yes. The direction of velocity continuously changes.

## PART 4 – FORCE AND LAWS OF MOTION

**Q76. What is force?**

**Answer:** A push or pull that can change or tend to change an object's state of motion or shape.

**Q77. What is the SI unit of force?**

**Answer:** Newton (N).

**Q78. What is the formula for force?**

**Answer:** F = ma.

**Q79. What is inertia?**

**Answer:** The tendency of an object to resist changes in its state of rest or motion.

**Q80. On what factor does inertia depend?**

**Answer:** Mass. Greater mass means greater inertia.

**Q81. What is Newton's First Law of Motion?**

**Answer:** An object remains at rest or in uniform straight-line motion unless acted upon by an external unbalanced force.

**Q82. Newton's First Law is also known as which law?**

**Answer:** Law of Inertia.

**Q83. What is Newton's Second Law of Motion?**

**Answer:** The rate of change of momentum is proportional to the applied force and occurs in its direction.

**Q84. What is the mathematical form of Newton's Second Law for constant mass?**

**Answer:** F = ma.

**Q85. What is Newton's Third Law of Motion?**

**Answer:** For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

**Q86. Why does a person move backward when a gun fires a bullet?**

**Answer:** Due to recoil, as the gun experiences an equal and opposite reaction.

**Q87. Why does a swimmer push water backward?**

**Answer:** Water pushes the swimmer forward with an equal and opposite force.

**Q88. What is momentum?**

**Answer:** The product of mass and velocity.

**Q89. What is the formula for momentum?**

**Answer:** p = mv.

**Q90. What is the SI unit of momentum?**

**Answer:** kg m/s.

**Q91. What is impulse?**

**Answer:** The change in momentum produced by a force acting over a time interval.

**Q92. What is the formula for impulse?**

**Answer:** Impulse = Force × Time = Change in momentum.

**Q93. What is the law of conservation of momentum?**

**Answer:** The total momentum of an isolated system remains constant if no external force acts on it.

**Q94. Why are seat belts used in vehicles?**

**Answer:** They increase the time taken to stop the body during a collision, reducing the impact force.

**Q95. Why do athletes run before taking a long jump?**

**Answer:** To gain greater momentum and kinetic energy.

## PART 5 – GRAVITATION AND PRESSURE

**Q96. What is gravitation?**

**Answer:** The universal force of attraction between any two masses.

**Q97. Who formulated the universal law of gravitation?**

**Answer:** Sir Isaac Newton.

**Q98. What is Newton's universal law of gravitation?**

**Answer:** Every two masses attract each other with a force proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centres.

**Q99. What is the formula for universal gravitational force?**

**Answer:** F = Gm₁m₂/r².

**Q100. What is the universal gravitational constant?**

**Answer:** G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m²/kg² approximately.

**Q101. What is acceleration due to gravity on Earth?**

**Answer:** Approximately 9.8 m/s².

**Q102. What is acceleration due to gravity on the Moon compared with Earth?**

**Answer:** Approximately one-sixth of Earth's value.

**Q103. Why is the weight of an object less on the Moon?**

**Answer:** Because the Moon's gravitational acceleration is approximately one-sixth of Earth's.

**Q104. Does the mass of an object change on the Moon?**

**Answer:** No. Mass remains constant.

**Q105. What is free fall?**

**Answer:** Motion of an object under the influence of gravity alone.

**Q106. What is the weight formula?**

**Answer:** W = mg.

**Q107. What is pressure?**

**Answer:** Force acting per unit area.

**Q108. What is the formula for pressure?**

**Answer:** Pressure = Force / Area.

**Q109. What is the SI unit of pressure?**

**Answer:** Pascal (Pa).

**Q110. Why do sharp knives cut more easily than blunt knives?**

**Answer:** Their smaller contact area produces greater pressure for the same applied force.

**Q111. Why do school bags have broad shoulder straps?**

**Answer:** To distribute weight over a larger area and reduce pressure.

**Q112. What is atmospheric pressure?**

**Answer:** The pressure exerted by the weight of the atmosphere.

**Q113. Which instrument measures atmospheric pressure?**

**Answer:** Barometer.

**Q114. Which instrument measures the pressure of a gas in a closed container?**

**Answer:** Pressure gauge or manometer, depending on the application.

**Q115. What is buoyant force?**

**Answer:** The upward force exerted by a fluid on an immersed or floating object.

**Q116. Who discovered the principle of buoyancy?**

**Answer:** Archimedes.

**Q117. What is Archimedes' principle?**

**Answer:** An immersed object experiences an upward force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by it.

**Q118. Why does an object float in water?**

**Answer:** It floats when the upward buoyant force balances its weight.

**Q119. Why does an iron ship float while an iron nail sinks?**

**Answer:** A ship's hollow shape gives it a large volume and lowers its average density, allowing it to displace enough water.

**Q120. What is relative density?**

**Answer:** The ratio of the density of a substance to the density of water at a specified reference temperature.

**Q121. Does relative density have a unit?**

**Answer:** No. It is dimensionless.

## PART 6 – WORK, ENERGY AND POWER

**Q122. What is work in Physics?**

**Answer:** Work is done when a force causes displacement in its direction or has a component along the displacement.

**Q123. What is the formula for work?**

**Answer:** W = Fs cos θ.

**Q124. What is the SI unit of work?**

**Answer:** Joule (J).

**Q125. When is work zero?**

**Answer:** When displacement is zero or when the force is perpendicular to displacement.

**Q126. What is energy?**

**Answer:** The capacity to do work.

**Q127. What is the SI unit of energy?**

**Answer:** Joule (J).

**Q128. What is kinetic energy?**

**Answer:** Energy possessed by an object due to its motion.

**Q129. What is the formula for kinetic energy?**

**Answer:** KE = ½mv².

**Q130. On which factors does kinetic energy depend?**

**Answer:** Mass and the square of velocity.

**Q131. What happens to kinetic energy if velocity doubles?**

**Answer:** It becomes four times.

**Q132. What happens to kinetic energy if mass doubles while velocity remains constant?**

**Answer:** It doubles.

**Q133. What is potential energy?**

**Answer:** Energy possessed by an object due to its position or configuration.

**Q134. What is gravitational potential energy near Earth's surface?**

**Answer:** PE = mgh.

**Q135. What is mechanical energy?**

**Answer:** The sum of kinetic energy and potential energy.

**Q136. What is the law of conservation of energy?**

**Answer:** Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it can only be transformed from one form to another.

**Q137. What is power?**

**Answer:** The rate of doing work or transferring energy.

**Q138. What is the formula for power?**

**Answer:** Power = Work / Time.

**Q139. What is the SI unit of power?**

**Answer:** Watt (W).

**Q140. What is one kilowatt equal to?**

**Answer:** 1,000 watts.

**Q141. What is one kilowatt-hour?**

**Answer:** The energy consumed by a 1 kW appliance operating for one hour.

**Q142. How many joules are present in one kilowatt-hour?**

**Answer:** 3.6 × 10⁶ J.

**Q143. Is kilowatt-hour a unit of power or energy?**

**Answer:** Energy.

**Q144. What is the commercial unit of electrical energy?**

**Answer:** Kilowatt-hour (kWh).

**Q145. What is horsepower?**

**Answer:** A non-SI unit of power. One mechanical horsepower is approximately 746 W.

## PART 7 – HEAT AND TEMPERATURE

**Q146. What is heat?**

**Answer:** Energy transferred from a hotter body to a colder body due to a temperature difference.

**Q147. What is temperature?**

**Answer:** A measure of the degree of hotness or coldness of a body.

**Q148. What is the SI unit of temperature?**

**Answer:** Kelvin (K).

**Q149. Which instrument measures temperature?**

**Answer:** Thermometer.

**Q150. What are the three modes of heat transfer?**

**Answer:** Conduction, convection and radiation.

**Q151. What is conduction?**

**Answer:** Transfer of heat through a substance without bulk movement of the substance.

**Q152. In which materials does conduction occur most effectively?**

**Answer:** Generally, metals.

**Q153. What is convection?**

**Answer:** Transfer of heat through the bulk movement of fluids such as liquids and gases.

**Q154. What is radiation?**

**Answer:** Transfer of heat through electromagnetic waves without requiring a material medium.

**Q155. Can heat radiation travel through a vacuum?**

**Answer:** Yes.

**Q156. Why do we feel heat from the Sun?**

**Answer:** Solar energy reaches Earth mainly through radiation.

**Q157. Why are cooking utensils generally made of metals?**

**Answer:** Metals are generally good conductors of heat.

**Q158. Why are handles of cooking utensils made of plastic or wood?**

**Answer:** They are poor conductors of heat.

**Q159. What is specific heat capacity?**

**Answer:** The heat required to raise the temperature of unit mass of a substance by one kelvin.

**Q160. What is the SI unit of specific heat capacity?**

**Answer:** J/(kg K).

**Q161. Which substance has a high specific heat capacity among common substances?**

**Answer:** Water.

**Q162. What is latent heat?**

**Answer:** Heat absorbed or released during a change of state without a change in temperature.

**Q163. What is the melting point of ice at normal atmospheric pressure?**

**Answer:** 0°C.

**Q164. What is the boiling point of water at normal atmospheric pressure?**

**Answer:** 100°C.

**Q165. What is the relationship between Celsius and Kelvin?**

**Answer:** K = °C + 273.15.

**Q166. What is the relationship between Celsius and Fahrenheit?**

**Answer:** °F = (9/5 × °C) + 32.

**Q167. Why does evaporation cause cooling?**

**Answer:** Higher-energy particles escape from the liquid, reducing the average kinetic energy of the remaining liquid.

**Q168. What is sublimation?**

**Answer:** Direct conversion of a solid into a gas without passing through the liquid state.

**Q169. Name two substances that undergo sublimation.**

**Answer:** Camphor and naphthalene.

**Q170. What is the greenhouse effect?**

**Answer:** Warming of Earth's surface and lower atmosphere due to absorption and re-emission of infrared radiation by greenhouse gases.

## PART 8 – LIGHT AND OPTICS

**Q171. What is light?**

**Answer:** A form of electromagnetic radiation that produces the sensation of vision.

**Q172. Does light require a medium to travel?**

**Answer:** No. Light can travel through a vacuum.

**Q173. What is the speed of light in vacuum?**

**Answer:** Approximately 3 × 10⁸ m/s.

**Q174. What is reflection of light?**

**Answer:** The bouncing back of light into the same medium after striking a surface.

**Q175. What is refraction of light?**

**Answer:** The change in direction of light when it passes from one transparent medium into another due to a change in speed.

**Q176. State the first law of reflection.**

**Answer:** The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection.

**Q177. State the second law of reflection.**

**Answer:** The incident ray, reflected ray and normal lie in the same plane.

**Q178. What type of image is formed by a plane mirror?**

**Answer:** Virtual, erect and same-sized.

**Q179. What is lateral inversion?**

**Answer:** The apparent interchange of left and right in a plane mirror.

**Q180. What is a concave mirror?**

**Answer:** A spherical mirror whose reflecting surface curves inward.

**Q181. What is a convex mirror?**

**Answer:** A spherical mirror whose reflecting surface bulges outward.

**Q182. Which mirror is used as a rear-view mirror in vehicles?**

**Answer:** Convex mirror.

**Q183. Why are convex mirrors used as rear-view mirrors?**

**Answer:** They provide a wider field of view and form erect, diminished images.

**Q184. Which mirror is used by dentists to view enlarged images of teeth?**

**Answer:** Concave mirror.

**Q185. Which mirror is used in torches and vehicle headlights?**

**Answer:** Concave mirror.

**Q186. What image is formed by a concave mirror when an object is beyond the centre of curvature?**

**Answer:** Real, inverted and diminished.

**Q187. What image is formed by a concave mirror when an object is at the centre of curvature?**

**Answer:** Real, inverted and of the same size.

**Q188. What image is formed by a concave mirror when an object is between the centre of curvature and focus?**

**Answer:** Real, inverted and enlarged.

**Q189. What image is formed by a concave mirror when an object is between the focus and pole?**

**Answer:** Virtual, erect and enlarged.

**Q190. What happens when an object is placed at the focus of a concave mirror?**

**Answer:** The reflected rays become parallel and the image is formed at infinity.

**Q191. What is a convex lens?**

**Answer:** A lens thicker at the centre than at the edges.

**Q192. What is a concave lens?**

**Answer:** A lens thinner at the centre than at the edges.

**Q193. Which lens is called a converging lens?**

**Answer:** Convex lens.

**Q194. Which lens is called a diverging lens?**

**Answer:** Concave lens.

**Q195. What type of image does a concave lens always form for a real object?**

**Answer:** Virtual, erect and diminished.

**Q196. What type of image is formed by a convex lens when an object is beyond 2F?**

**Answer:** Real, inverted and diminished, between F and 2F.

**Q197. What type of image is formed by a convex lens when an object is at 2F?**

**Answer:** Real, inverted and same-sized, at 2F on the other side.

**Q198. What type of image is formed by a convex lens when an object is between F and 2F?**

**Answer:** Real, inverted and enlarged, beyond 2F.

**Q199. What type of image is formed by a convex lens when an object is between the optical centre and focus?**

**Answer:** Virtual, erect and enlarged.

**Q200. What happens when an object is placed at the focus of a convex lens?**

**Answer:** The refracted rays become parallel and the image is formed at infinity.

**Q201. What is the focal length of a lens?**

**Answer:** The distance between its optical centre and principal focus.

**Q202. What is the power of a lens?**

**Answer:** The reciprocal of its focal length in metres.

**Q203. What is the formula for power of a lens?**

**Answer:** P = 1/f.

**Q204. What is the unit of lens power?**

**Answer:** Dioptre (D).

**Q205. What is the power of a convex lens?**

**Answer:** Positive.

**Q206. What is the power of a concave lens?**

**Answer:** Negative.

**Q207. Which lens corrects myopia?**

**Answer:** Concave lens.

**Q208. Which lens corrects hypermetropia?**

**Answer:** Convex lens.

**Q209. What is myopia?**

**Answer:** Near-sightedness, in which distant objects appear blurred.

**Q210. What is hypermetropia?**

**Answer:** Far-sightedness, in which nearby objects appear blurred.

**Q211. What is presbyopia?**

**Answer:** An age-related defect in which the eye loses some ability to focus on nearby objects.

**Q212. Which lens is generally used to correct presbyopia?**

**Answer:** Convex lens or bifocal/progressive lenses, depending on the person's vision needs.

**Q213. What is dispersion of light?**

**Answer:** Splitting of white light into its constituent colours.

**Q214. Which device demonstrates dispersion of white light?**

**Answer:** Glass prism.

**Q215. How many colours are traditionally identified in the visible spectrum?**

**Answer:** Seven.

**Q216. Name the seven colours of the visible spectrum.**

**Answer:** Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange and Red.

**Q217. Which colour deviates the most in a glass prism?**

**Answer:** Violet.

**Q218. Which colour deviates the least in a glass prism?**

**Answer:** Red.

**Q219. Why does the sky appear blue?**

**Answer:** Due to scattering of shorter wavelengths of sunlight by molecules in the atmosphere.

**Q220. Why does the Sun appear reddish at sunrise and sunset?**

**Answer:** Shorter wavelengths are scattered away over the longer atmospheric path, allowing more red and orange light to reach the observer.

**Q221. Why does the Sun appear nearly white at noon?**

**Answer:** Sunlight travels through a shorter atmospheric path, so less selective scattering occurs.

**Q222. What is the Tyndall effect?**

**Answer:** Scattering of light by colloidal particles, making the path of light visible.

## PART 10 – SOUND

**Q233. What is sound?**

**Answer:** A form of energy produced by vibrations that travels through a medium as a mechanical wave.

**Q234. Does sound require a medium to travel?**

**Answer:** Yes.

**Q235. Can sound travel through a vacuum?**

**Answer:** No.

**Q236. What type of wave is sound in air?**

**Answer:** Longitudinal wave.

**Q237. What are compressions in a sound wave?**

**Answer:** Regions of high pressure and high density.

**Q238. What are rarefactions in a sound wave?**

**Answer:** Regions of low pressure and low density.

**Q239. What is frequency?**

**Answer:** The number of complete vibrations or oscillations per second.

**Q240. What is the SI unit of frequency?**

**Answer:** Hertz (Hz).

**Q241. What is amplitude?**

**Answer:** The maximum displacement of a vibrating particle from its mean position.

**Q242. What determines the loudness of sound?**

**Answer:** Mainly the amplitude or intensity of the sound wave.

**Q243. What determines the pitch of sound?**

**Answer:** Frequency.

**Q244. What is the audible frequency range of a healthy human ear?**

**Answer:** Approximately 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz.

**Q245. What is infrasonic sound?**

**Answer:** Sound with frequency below 20 Hz.

**Q246. What is ultrasonic sound?**

**Answer:** Sound with frequency above 20,000 Hz.

**Q247. Which animals can hear ultrasonic sounds?**

**Answer:** Bats and dogs.

**Q248. Which animals can detect infrasonic sounds?**

**Answer:** Elephants and some whales.

**Q249. What is the approximate speed of sound in air at room temperature?**

**Answer:** Approximately 343 m/s at 20°C.

**Q250. In which medium does sound generally travel fastest: solids, liquids or gases?**

**Answer:** Solids.

**Q251. What is an echo?**

**Answer:** Repetition of sound caused by reflection from a distant surface.

**Q252. What is the minimum time gap generally required to hear a distinct echo?**

**Answer:** Approximately 0.1 second.

**Q253. What is the minimum distance of a reflecting surface for a distinct echo in air at room temperature?**

**Answer:** Approximately 17 metres.

**Q254. What is reverberation?**

**Answer:** Persistence of sound due to repeated reflections after the original sound stops.

**Q255. What is SONAR?**

**Answer:** Sound Navigation and Ranging, a technique that uses ultrasonic waves to detect underwater objects and measure distances.

**Q256. What is the Doppler effect?**

**Answer:** The apparent change in frequency of a wave due to relative motion between the source and observer.

**Q257. What is the function of the eardrum?**

**Answer:** It vibrates in response to sound waves and transfers vibrations to the middle-ear bones.

**Q259. What are the two types of electric charge?**

**Q260. What happens when two like charges are brought near each other?**

**Q261. What happens when two unlike charges are brought near each other?**

**Answer:** They attract each other.

**Q262. What is the SI unit of electric charge?**

**Answer:** Coulomb (C).

**Q263. What is the charge of an electron?**

**Answer:** −1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C approximately.

**Q264. What is the charge of a proton?**

**Answer:** +1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C approximately.

**Q265. What is electric current?**

**Answer:** The rate of flow of electric charge.

**Q266. What is the formula for electric current?**

**Answer:** I = Q/t.

**Q267. What is the SI unit of electric current?**

**Answer:** Ampere (A).

**Q268. What is the conventional direction of electric current in an external circuit?**

**Answer:** From the positive terminal to the negative terminal.

**Q269. In which direction do electrons flow in a metallic conductor?**

**Answer:** From the negative terminal to the positive terminal.

**Q270. What is potential difference?**

**Answer:** The work done per unit charge to move a charge between two points.

**Q271. What is the formula for potential difference?**

**Answer:** V = W/Q.

**Q272. What is the SI unit of potential difference?**

**Answer:** Volt (V).

**Q273. What is electrical resistance?**

**Answer:** The opposition offered by a conductor to the flow of electric current.

**Q274. What is the SI unit of resistance?**

**Answer:** Ohm (Ω).

**Q275. State Ohm's law.**

**Answer:** At constant temperature and other physical conditions, current through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across it.

**Q276. What is the mathematical form of Ohm's law?**

**Answer:** V = IR.

**Q277. What is a conductor?**

**Answer:** A material that allows electric current to flow relatively easily.

**Q278. Give examples of electrical conductors.**

**Answer:** Copper, aluminium, silver and iron.

**Q279. What is an insulator?**

**Answer:** A material that strongly resists the flow of electric current.

**Q280. Give examples of electrical insulators.**

**Answer:** Rubber, glass, plastic and dry wood.

**Q281. What is resistivity?**

**Answer:** An intrinsic property of a material that describes its opposition to electric current.

**Q282. What is the SI unit of resistivity?**

**Answer:** Ohm metre (Ω m).

**Q283. What is the formula for resistance of a uniform conductor?**

**Answer:** R = ρL/A.

**Q284. On which factors does resistance depend?**

**Answer:** Length, cross-sectional area, material and temperature.

**Q285. How does resistance change when the length of a wire increases?**

**Answer:** Resistance increases.

**Q286. How does resistance change when the cross-sectional area of a wire increases?**

**Answer:** Resistance decreases.

**Q287. What is the formula for resistors connected in series?**

**Answer:** R = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ + ...

**Q288. What is the formula for two resistors connected in parallel?**

**Answer:** 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂.

**Q289. What happens to equivalent resistance when resistors are connected in series?**

**Answer:** It increases.

**Q290. What happens to equivalent resistance when resistors are connected in parallel?**

**Answer:** It is less than the smallest individual resistance.

**Q291. Why are household electrical appliances connected in parallel?**

**Answer:** Each appliance receives the same supply voltage and can operate independently.

**Q292. What is electric power?**

**Answer:** The rate at which electrical energy is consumed or converted.

**Q293. What is the formula for electrical power?**

**Answer:** P = VI.

**Q294. Give two other formulas for electrical power using Ohm's law.**

**Answer:** P = I²R and P = V²/R.

**Q295. What is the heating effect of electric current?**

**Answer:** The production of heat when electric current flows through a resistance.

**Q296. State Joule's law of heating.**

**Answer:** Heat produced is proportional to the square of current, resistance and time.

**Q297. What is the formula for Joule's heating?**

**Answer:** H = I²Rt.

**Q298. Which device protects an electric circuit from excessive current?**

**Answer:** Fuse or circuit breaker.

**Q299. What is the function of an electric fuse?**

**Answer:** It melts and breaks the circuit when excessive current flows.

**Q300. What material is commonly used in traditional fuse wire?**

**Answer:** A low-melting-point alloy, traditionally made from tin and lead.

**Q301. What is a short circuit?**

**Answer:** An unintended low-resistance path that allows excessive current to flow.

**Q302. What is overloading?**

**Answer:** Drawing more current from a circuit than its safe capacity.

**Q303. What is earthing?**

**Answer:** Connecting the exposed metallic body of electrical equipment to the ground through a protective conductor.

**Q304. Why is earthing important?**

**Answer:** It provides a low-resistance path for fault current and helps prevent electric shock.

**Q305. What is the commercial unit of electrical energy?**

**Answer:** Kilowatt-hour (kWh).

## PART 12 – MAGNETISM AND ELECTROMAGNETISM

**Q306. What is a magnet?**

**Answer:** An object that produces a magnetic field and attracts certain magnetic materials.

**Q307. What are the two poles of a magnet?**

**Answer:** North pole and South pole.

**Q308. What happens when like magnetic poles are brought together?**

**Answer:** They repel each other.

**Q309. What happens when unlike magnetic poles are brought together?**

**Answer:** They attract each other.

**Q310. Can a magnetic pole exist independently?**

**Answer:** No. A magnet always has both north and south poles.

**Q311. Which materials are strongly attracted by magnets?**

**Answer:** Iron, nickel and cobalt.

**Q312. What is a magnetic field?**

**Answer:** The region around a magnet or current-carrying conductor where magnetic forces can be experienced.

**Q313. What are magnetic field lines?**

**Answer:** Imaginary lines representing the direction and pattern of a magnetic field.

**Q314. Where are magnetic field lines closest together?**

**Answer:** Near the poles, where the magnetic field is strongest.

**Q315. Who discovered the magnetic effect of electric current?**

**Answer:** Hans Christian Oersted.

**Q316. What is an electromagnet?**

**Answer:** A temporary magnet produced by passing electric current through a coil, usually wound around a soft iron core.

**Q317. Which material is commonly used as the core of an electromagnet?**

**Answer:** Soft iron.

**Q318. What happens to the magnetic field of a coil when current increases?**

**Answer:** Its magnetic field becomes stronger.

**Q319. What is an electric motor?**

**Answer:** A device that converts electrical energy into mechanical energy.

**Q320. What is an electric generator?**

**Answer:** A device that converts mechanical energy into electrical energy.

**Q321. What is electromagnetic induction?**

**Answer:** The production of an electromotive force when the magnetic flux linked with a circuit changes.

**Q322. Who discovered electromagnetic induction?**

**Answer:** Michael Faraday.

**Q323. What is the principle of an electric generator?**

**Answer:** Electromagnetic induction.

**Q324. What is the principle of an electric motor?**

**Answer:** A current-carrying conductor experiences a force in a magnetic field.

**Q325. What is the function of a split-ring commutator in a DC motor?**

**Answer:** It reverses the current direction in the coil every half rotation, maintaining rotation in the same direction.

**Q326. What is the function of a slip ring in an AC generator?**

**Answer:** It transfers the generated alternating current from the rotating coil to the external circuit.

**Q327. What is alternating current (AC)?**

**Answer:** Electric current that periodically changes its magnitude and direction.

**Q328. What is direct current (DC)?**

**Answer:** Electric current that flows in one direction.

**Q329. What is the standard frequency of AC mains supply in India?**

**Answer:** 50 Hz.

**Q330. What is the approximate domestic single-phase supply voltage in India?**

**Answer:** 230 V nominal.

**Q331. What is a transformer?**

**Answer:** A device that changes alternating voltage using electromagnetic induction.

**Q332. What is a step-up transformer?**

**Answer:** A transformer that increases voltage and decreases current for an ideal transformer.

**Q333. What is a step-down transformer?**

**Answer:** A transformer that decreases voltage and increases current for an ideal transformer.

**Q334. Can an ordinary transformer operate directly on steady DC?**

**Answer:** No. A transformer requires changing magnetic flux.

## PART 16 – APPLICATION-BASED PHYSICS

**Q396. Why do passengers fall forward when a moving bus suddenly stops?**

**Answer:** Due to inertia of motion.

**Q397. Why do passengers fall backward when a stationary bus suddenly starts?**

**Answer:** Due to inertia of rest.

**Q398. Why does a gun recoil after firing a bullet?**

**Answer:** Due to conservation of momentum and Newton's Third Law.

**Q399. Why does a cyclist lean inward while taking a turn?**

**Answer:** To maintain balance and provide the required centripetal force.

**Q400. Why are roads banked at sharp turns?**

**Answer:** To help provide the required centripetal force and reduce dependence on friction.

**Q401. Why does a stone tied to a string move in a circular path?**

**Answer:** The string provides centripetal force.

**Q402. What happens if the string of a rotating stone breaks?**

**Answer:** The stone moves along the tangent to the circular path at the point of release.

**Q403. Why does a pressure cooker cook food faster?**

**Answer:** Increased pressure raises the boiling point of water, allowing food to cook at a higher temperature.

**Q404. Why do clouds appear white?**

**Answer:** Water droplets and ice crystals scatter visible wavelengths of sunlight.

**Q405. Why do stars appear to twinkle?**

**Answer:** Due to atmospheric refraction of starlight.

**Q406. Why do planets generally not twinkle as much as stars?**

**Answer:** Planets appear as extended sources, so fluctuations in light from different parts tend to average out.

**Q407. Why is lightning seen before thunder is heard?**

**Answer:** Light travels much faster than sound.

**Q408. Why are railway tracks provided with gaps or expansion arrangements?**

**Answer:** To allow for thermal expansion.

**Q409. Why does a metal spoon feel colder than a wooden spoon at the same temperature?**

**Answer:** Metal conducts heat away from the hand faster than wood.

**Q410. Why does ice float on water?**

**Answer:** Ice has lower density than liquid water.

**Q411. Why are dams made thicker at the bottom?**

**Answer:** Water pressure increases with depth.

**Q412. Why does a hydraulic lift work?**

**Answer:** It works on Pascal's law.

**Q413. State Pascal's law.**

**Answer:** Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally and undiminished in all directions.

**Q414. Which principle is used in hydraulic brakes?**

**Answer:** Pascal's law.

**Q415. Why do objects appear bent when partly immersed in water?**

**Answer:** Due to refraction of light.

**Q416. Why does a coin at the bottom of water appear raised?**

**Answer:** Due to refraction of light at the water-air boundary.

**Q417. Why are optical fibres used in communication?**

**Answer:** They transmit light signals using total internal reflection.

**Q418. What is total internal reflection?**

**Answer:** Complete reflection of light back into a denser medium when the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle.

**Q419. What is the function of a lightning conductor?**

**Answer:** It provides a safe conducting path for lightning current to reach the ground.

**Q420. Why is a fuse connected in series with an appliance?**

**Answer:** So that excessive current passes through it and breaks the circuit when it melts.

**Q421. Why are electric transmission lines operated at high voltage?**

**Answer:** To reduce current and minimise power losses in transmission.

**Q422. Why is copper commonly used for electrical wiring?**

**Answer:** It has high electrical conductivity and is ductile.

**Q423. Why is tungsten used in traditional incandescent bulb filaments?**

**Answer:** It has a very high melting point.

**Q424. Why does an electric bulb filament glow?**

**Answer:** It becomes extremely hot due to the heating effect of electric current and emits light.

**Q425. Why does a transformer have a laminated iron core?**

**Answer:** To reduce energy loss due to eddy currents.

**Q426. Why is soft iron used in electromagnets?**

**Answer:** It is easily magnetised and demagnetised.

**Q427. Why is steel used for permanent magnets?**

**Answer:** It retains magnetism more effectively than soft iron.

**Q428. Why do magnetic field lines never intersect?**

**Answer:** Because the magnetic field at a point cannot have two different directions simultaneously.

**Q429. Why does an electric motor rotate?**

**Answer:** A current-carrying coil experiences a torque in a magnetic field.

**Q430. Why does an electric generator produce current?**

**Answer:** Changing magnetic flux induces an electromotive force in the coil.
