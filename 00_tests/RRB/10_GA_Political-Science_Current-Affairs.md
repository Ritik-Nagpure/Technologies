# SECTION A — INDIAN POLITICAL SCIENCE

## 1. Parliamentary Procedures

Q1. How many sessions does the Indian Parliament normally conduct in a year?
A. Three: Budget Session, Monsoon Session and Winter Session.

Q2. Who has the power to summon each House of Parliament?
A. The President.

Q3. What is the maximum permissible gap between two sessions of Parliament?
A. Six months.

Q4. What is an adjournment?
A. Suspension of a sitting of the House for a specified period.

Q5. What is adjournment sine die?
A. Ending a sitting without specifying the date for the next sitting.

Q6. What is prorogation?
A. Formal termination of a session of Parliament by the President.

Q7. What is dissolution of Lok Sabha?
A. The termination of the life of the Lok Sabha before or at the end of its normal term.

Q8. Which House of Parliament cannot be dissolved?
A. Rajya Sabha.

Q9. What is the quorum required to conduct a meeting of either House of Parliament?
A. One-tenth of the total membership of that House.

Q10. What is Question Hour?
A. The period during which members ask questions to ministers about government activities.

Q11. What is Zero Hour?
A. The period after Question Hour when members raise matters of urgent public importance.

Q12. Is Zero Hour mentioned in the Constitution?
A. No.

Q13. What is a starred question?
A. A question that requires an oral answer and allows supplementary questions.

Q14. What is an unstarred question?
A. A question answered in writing, without supplementary questions.

Q15. What is a short-notice question?
A. A question asked with shorter notice than normally prescribed on an urgent matter.

Q16. What is a No-Confidence Motion?
A. A motion expressing that the Lok Sabha no longer has confidence in the Council of Ministers.

Q17. In which House can a No-Confidence Motion be introduced?
A. Lok Sabha only.

Q18. What happens if a No-Confidence Motion is passed?
A. The Council of Ministers must resign.

Q19. What is a Censure Motion?
A. A motion criticising a specific government policy or action.

Q20. What is an Adjournment Motion?
A. A motion used to discuss a definite matter of urgent public importance by interrupting normal business.

Q21. What is a Privilege Motion?
A. A motion concerning an alleged breach of the privileges of Parliament or its members.

Q22. What is a Calling Attention Motion?
A. A procedure through which a member draws a minister's attention to an urgent public matter.

Q23. What is a Cut Motion?
A. A motion moved in Lok Sabha to reduce a demand for grants.

Q24. What is a Guillotine in Parliament?
A. The process of putting outstanding demands for grants to vote without further discussion after the allotted time expires.

Q25. What is a Private Members' Day?
A. A designated parliamentary period for discussing business introduced by members who are not ministers.

## 2. Parliamentary Majorities

Q26. What is a simple majority?
A. More than half of the members present and voting.

Q27. What is an absolute majority?
A. More than half of the total sanctioned membership of the House.

Q28. What is an effective majority?
A. More than half of the effective strength of the House, excluding vacancies.

Q29. What is a special majority?
A. A constitutionally prescribed higher majority, generally requiring two-thirds of members present and voting along with a majority of the total membership.

Q30. Which majority is generally required to remove the Vice-President?
A. A majority of all the then members of Rajya Sabha, followed by agreement of Lok Sabha.

Q31. Which majority is required to remove the Speaker of Lok Sabha?
A. A majority of all the then members of Lok Sabha.

Q32. Which majority is required to pass a normal motion in Parliament?
A. Generally, a simple majority.

Q33. What is a Parliamentary Committee?
A. A committee of Parliament members formed to examine specific legislative, financial or administrative matters.

Q34. What is the main function of the Public Accounts Committee?

Q35. How many members are there in the Public Accounts Committee?

Q36. What is the main function of the Estimates Committee?

Q37. How many members are in the Estimates Committee?

Q38. What is the main function of the Committee on Public Undertakings?

Q39. How many members are in the Committee on Public Undertakings?
A. 22: 15 from Lok Sabha and 7 from Rajya Sabha.

Q40. Which committee examines government assurances given to Parliament?
A. Committee on Government Assurances.

Q41. Which committee examines the ethical conduct of members?
A. Ethics Committee.

Q42. What is a Department-related Standing Committee?
A. A parliamentary committee that examines the work, demands for grants and Bills relating to specific ministries or departments.

## 4. President and Governor — Powers

Q43. What are the three types of veto powers of the Indian President?
A. Absolute veto, suspensive veto and pocket veto.

Q44. What is an absolute veto?
A. The President withholds assent to a Bill.

Q45. What is a suspensive veto?
A. The President returns an ordinary Bill for reconsideration.

Q46. What is a pocket veto?
A. The President neither grants nor refuses assent to a Bill for an indefinite period.

Q47. Can the President return a Money Bill for reconsideration?
A. No.

Q48. Which Article gives the President the power to grant pardons?
A. Article 72.

Q49. Which Article gives the Governor the power to grant pardons?
A. Article 161.

Q50. Can the Governor pardon a death sentence?
A. No. The Governor can suspend, remit or commute a death sentence, but the President has the power to pardon it.

Q51. Who appoints the Chief Minister of a state?
A. The Governor.

Q52. What is the normal term of a Governor?
A. Five years, subject to holding office during the pleasure of the President.

Q53. What is the minimum age required to become a Governor?
A. 35 years.

Q54. Can a Governor reserve a State Bill for the consideration of the President?
A. Yes.

Q55. Who appoints the Advocate General of a state?
A. The Governor.

Q56. Which Article deals with the Advocate General of a state?
A. Article 165.

## 5. Centre–State Relations

Q57. Which Schedule distributes legislative subjects between the Union and the States?
A. Seventh Schedule.

Q58. How many lists are included in the Seventh Schedule?

Q59. What are the three legislative lists?

Q60. Which government has exclusive legislative power over the Union List?

Q61. Which government normally legislates on the State List?

A. Subjects in the Concurrent List.

Q63. What happens when a Union law and a State law conflict on a Concurrent List subject?

Q64. Who has residuary legislative powers in India?

Q66. Which Article provides for the Inter-State Council?

Q71. Which Article deals with the distribution of taxes between the Union and States?

Q72. What is the purpose of the Finance Commission's tax devolution recommendations?

## 8. Writs and Judicial Concepts

Q105. How many types of constitutional writs are recognised in India?
A. Five.

Q106. What is Habeas Corpus?
A. A writ requiring a detained person to be produced before a court to examine the legality of detention.

Q107. What is Mandamus?
A. A writ directing a public authority to perform a legal duty.

Q108. What is Prohibition?
A. A writ issued by a higher court preventing a lower court or tribunal from exceeding its jurisdiction.

Q109. What is Certiorari?
A. A writ through which a higher court quashes an order of a lower court or tribunal for jurisdictional or legal error.

Q110. What is Quo Warranto?
A. A writ questioning the legal authority of a person holding a public office.

Q111. Which courts can issue writs under the Constitution?
A. Supreme Court and High Courts.

Q112. Which Article gives High Courts the power to issue writs?
A. Article 226.

Q113. Which doctrine protects the basic features of the Constitution from being destroyed by amendments?
A. Basic Structure Doctrine.

Q114. Which Supreme Court case established the Basic Structure Doctrine?
A. Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala, 1973.

Q115. What is judicial review?
A. The power of courts to examine whether laws and executive actions comply with the Constitution.

Q116. What is Public Interest Litigation?
A. A legal proceeding initiated to protect public interest, especially where affected persons may face difficulty approaching the courts themselves.
