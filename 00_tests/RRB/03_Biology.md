# PART 2 – CELL BIOLOGY

**Q13. [Easy]** What is the basic structural and functional unit of life?

**Answer:** Cell.

**Q14. [Easy]** Who discovered cells?

**Answer:** Robert Hooke in 1665.

**Q15. [Easy]** Who first observed living cells?

**Answer:** Antonie van Leeuwenhoek.

**Q16. [Easy]** Who proposed the cell theory?

**Answer:** Matthias Schleiden and Theodor Schwann.

**Q17. [Easy]** Who stated that all cells arise from pre-existing cells?

**Answer:** Rudolf Virchow.

**Q18. [Easy]** What are the three main components of a typical cell?

**Answer:** Cell membrane, cytoplasm and nucleus.

**Q19. [Easy]** What is the outer boundary of an animal cell called?

**Answer:** Plasma membrane or cell membrane.

**Q20. [Easy]** What is the function of the cell membrane?

**Answer:** It controls the movement of substances into and out of the cell.

**Q21. [Easy]** What is the cell wall?

**Answer:** A rigid outer covering present in plant cells, mainly composed of cellulose.

**Q22. [Easy]** Which structure is present in plant cells but absent in animal cells?

**Answer:** Cell wall.

**Q23. [Easy]** What is cytoplasm?

**Answer:** The jelly-like material inside the cell membrane that contains cell organelles.

**Q24. [Easy]** Which organelle is known as the powerhouse of the cell?

**Answer:** Mitochondria.

**Q25. [Easy]** What is the main function of mitochondria?

**Answer:** Production of ATP through cellular respiration.

**Q26. [Easy]** Which organelle is known as the kitchen of the cell?

**Answer:** Chloroplast.

**Q27. [Easy]** What is the main function of chloroplasts?

**Answer:** Photosynthesis.

**Q28. [Easy]** Which pigment gives plants their green colour?

**Answer:** Chlorophyll.

**Q29. [Easy]** Which organelle controls most activities of a eukaryotic cell?

**Answer:** Nucleus.

**Q30. [Easy]** What is the function of chromosomes?

**Answer:** They carry genetic information in the form of DNA.

**Q31. [Easy]** What is DNA?

**Answer:** Deoxyribonucleic acid, the hereditary material of most living organisms.

**Q32. [Easy]** What is RNA?

**Answer:** Ribonucleic acid, which plays important roles in protein synthesis and gene expression.

**Q33. [Moderate]** Which organelle is known as the suicidal bag of the cell?

**Answer:** Lysosome.

**Q34. [Moderate]** What is the function of lysosomes?

**Answer:** Intracellular digestion and breakdown of waste materials and worn-out cell components.

**Q35. [Moderate]** Which organelle is responsible for protein synthesis?

**Answer:** Ribosome.

**Q36. [Moderate]** Which cell organelle modifies, packages and transports proteins?

**Answer:** Golgi apparatus.

**Q37. [Moderate]** What is the function of the endoplasmic reticulum?

**Answer:** It helps in the synthesis and transport of proteins and lipids.

**Q38. [Moderate]** Which type of endoplasmic reticulum has ribosomes?

**Answer:** Rough endoplasmic reticulum.

**Q39. [Moderate]** Which type of endoplasmic reticulum is involved in lipid synthesis?

**Answer:** Smooth endoplasmic reticulum.

**Q40. [Moderate]** Which organelle stores water, food and waste materials in plant cells?

**Answer:** Vacuole.

**Q41. [Moderate]** Which organelle helps in cell division in animal cells?

**Answer:** Centriole.

**Q42. [Moderate]** What is osmosis?

**Answer:** Movement of water through a selectively permeable membrane from higher water potential to lower water potential.

**Q43. [Moderate]** What is diffusion?

**Answer:** Movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration.

**Q44. [Moderate]** What is plasmolysis?

**Answer:** Shrinkage of the cell contents away from the cell wall due to loss of water in a hypertonic solution.

**Q45. [Moderate]** What is the difference between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells?

**Answer:** Prokaryotic cells lack a membrane-bound nucleus and membrane-bound organelles; eukaryotic cells possess them.

**Q46. [Moderate]** Give an example of a prokaryotic organism.

**Answer:** Bacteria.

**Q47. [Moderate]** Give examples of eukaryotic organisms.

**Answer:** Animals, plants, fungi and protists.

**Q48. [Difficult]** Which cell organelle contains its own DNA apart from the nucleus?

**Answer:** Mitochondria and chloroplasts.

**Q49. [Difficult]** What is the function of ATP?

**Answer:** ATP stores and transfers usable energy for cellular activities.

**Q50. [Difficult]** What is the difference between mitosis and meiosis?

**Answer:** Mitosis generally produces two genetically similar daughter cells; meiosis produces four haploid cells after two divisions.

# PART 3 – CELL DIVISION AND GENETICS

**Q51. [Easy]** What is cell division?

**Answer:** The process by which a parent cell divides to form daughter cells.

**Q52. [Easy]** What is mitosis?

**Answer:** A type of cell division that produces two genetically similar daughter cells with the same chromosome number as the parent cell.

**Q53. [Easy]** What is meiosis?

**Answer:** A type of cell division that reduces the chromosome number by half and produces haploid cells.

**Q54. [Easy]** Where does meiosis occur in humans?

**Answer:** In the reproductive organs during the formation of gametes.

**Q55. [Easy]** What is a gene?

**Answer:** A segment of DNA that contains information for a functional product, such as a protein or RNA.

**Q56. [Easy]** What are chromosomes?

**Answer:** Structures made of DNA and proteins that carry genes.

**Q57. [Easy]** How many chromosomes are present in a normal human body cell?

**Answer:** 46 chromosomes or 23 pairs.

**Q58. [Easy]** How many chromosomes are present in a normal human sperm or egg cell?

**Answer:** 23 chromosomes.

**Q59. [Easy]** What is heredity?

**Answer:** Transmission of characteristics from parents to offspring.

**Q60. [Easy]** Who is known as the father of genetics?

**Answer:** Gregor Johann Mendel.

**Q61. [Easy]** On which plant did Mendel conduct his famous experiments?

**Answer:** Garden pea plant.

**Q62. [Moderate]** What is a dominant trait?

**Answer:** A trait expressed in a heterozygous individual.

**Q63. [Moderate]** What is a recessive trait?

**Answer:** A trait that is generally expressed only when two recessive alleles are present in a diploid organism.

**Q64. [Moderate]** What is genotype?

**Answer:** The genetic constitution of an organism.

**Q65. [Moderate]** What is phenotype?

**Answer:** The observable characteristics of an organism.

**Q66. [Moderate]** What is a homozygous individual?

**Answer:** An individual having two identical alleles for a gene.

**Q67. [Moderate]** What is a heterozygous individual?

**Answer:** An individual having two different alleles for a gene.

**Q68. [Moderate]** What is the phenotypic ratio in Mendel's typical monohybrid F2 cross with complete dominance?

**Answer:** 3:1.

**Q69. [Moderate]** What is the genotypic ratio in a typical monohybrid F2 cross?

**Answer:** 1:2:1.

**Q70. [Moderate]** What determines the biological sex of a child in humans?

**Answer:** The combination of sex chromosomes contributed by the parents. The sperm contributes either X or Y; the egg normally contributes X.

**Q71. [Moderate]** What are the sex chromosomes of a typical human female?

**Answer:** XX.

**Q72. [Moderate]** What are the sex chromosomes of a typical human male?

**Answer:** XY.

**Q73. [Moderate]** Who determines whether a typical human child is genetically male or female?

**Answer:** The father, because sperm may carry either an X or a Y chromosome.

**Q74. [Difficult]** What is mutation?

**Answer:** A change in the DNA sequence.

**Q75. [Difficult]** What is evolution?

**Answer:** Change in heritable characteristics of populations across generations.

**Q76. [Difficult]** Who proposed the theory of natural selection?

**Answer:** Charles Darwin and Alfred Russel Wallace independently developed the idea; Darwin published the influential explanation in 1859.

# PART 4 – HUMAN DIGESTIVE SYSTEM

**Q77. [Easy]** What is digestion?

**Answer:** The breakdown of complex food into simpler substances that can be absorbed.

**Q78. [Easy]** Where does digestion begin in humans?

**Answer:** Mouth.

**Q79. [Easy]** Which enzyme is present in saliva?

**Answer:** Salivary amylase or ptyalin.

**Q80. [Easy]** What is the function of salivary amylase?

**Answer:** It begins the breakdown of starch into simpler sugars, mainly maltose and shorter carbohydrates.

**Q81. [Easy]** Which organ produces bile?

**Answer:** Liver.

**Q82. [Easy]** Where is bile stored?

**Answer:** Gallbladder.

**Q83. [Easy]** What is the function of bile?

**Answer:** It emulsifies fats and helps create an alkaline environment for digestion in the small intestine.

**Q84. [Easy]** Which organ produces hydrochloric acid in the digestive system?

**Answer:** Stomach.

**Q85. [Easy]** What is the function of hydrochloric acid in the stomach?

**Answer:** It creates an acidic environment, helps activate pepsin and kills many microbes.

**Q86. [Easy]** Which enzyme digests proteins in the stomach?

**Answer:** Pepsin.

**Q87. [Easy]** Which organ produces pancreatic juice?

**Answer:** Pancreas.

**Q88. [Easy]** Where does most digestion and absorption of nutrients occur?

**Answer:** Small intestine.

**Q89. [Easy]** What are villi?

**Answer:** Small finger-like projections lining the small intestine that increase the surface area for absorption.

**Q90. [Easy]** Which part of the digestive system absorbs most water from undigested food?

**Answer:** Large intestine.

**Q91. [Easy]** What is the function of the rectum?

**Answer:** Temporary storage of faeces before elimination.

**Q92. [Moderate]** Which enzyme digests fats?

**Answer:** Lipase.

**Q93. [Moderate]** Which enzyme breaks down proteins into smaller peptides in the stomach?

**Answer:** Pepsin.

**Q94. [Moderate]** Which enzyme converts starch into maltose?

**Answer:** Amylase.

**Q95. [Moderate]** Which enzyme breaks down maltose into glucose?

**Answer:** Maltase.

**Q96. [Moderate]** Which enzyme breaks down proteins into amino acids in the small intestine?

**Answer:** Peptidases, which complete the breakdown of peptides into amino acids.

**Q97. [Moderate]** What is peristalsis?

**Answer:** Wave-like muscular contractions that move food through the digestive tract.

**Q98. [Moderate]** What is the longest part of the human digestive tract?

**Answer:** Small intestine.

**Q99. [Moderate]** Which organ produces insulin and digestive enzymes?

**Answer:** Pancreas.

**Q100. [Moderate]** What is the main function of the liver in digestion?

**Answer:** Production of bile.

**Q101. [Difficult]** What is the approximate pH of the stomach?

**Answer:** Generally around 1.5–3.5, depending on conditions.

**Q102. [Difficult]** What is the function of the epiglottis?

**Answer:** It helps prevent food from entering the windpipe during swallowing.

# PART 5 – HUMAN RESPIRATORY SYSTEM

**Q103. [Easy]** What is respiration?

**Answer:** The process by which cells release energy from nutrients.

**Q104. [Easy]** What is breathing?

**Answer:** The physical process of inhaling and exhaling air.

**Q105. [Easy]** Which organs are responsible for breathing in humans?

**Answer:** Lungs.

**Q106. [Easy]** What is the main site of gas exchange in human lungs?

**Answer:** Alveoli.

**Q107. [Easy]** What are alveoli?

**Answer:** Tiny air sacs in the lungs where oxygen and carbon dioxide are exchanged.

**Q108. [Easy]** Which gas is absorbed into the blood during respiration?

**Answer:** Oxygen.

**Q109. [Easy]** Which gas is removed from the blood and exhaled?

**Answer:** Carbon dioxide.

**Q110. [Easy]** Which respiratory pigment transports oxygen in human blood?

**Answer:** Haemoglobin.

**Q111. [Easy]** Where is haemoglobin present?

**Answer:** Red blood cells.

**Q112. [Easy]** Which muscle plays a major role in breathing?

**Answer:** Diaphragm.

**Q113. [Easy]** What happens to the diaphragm during inhalation?

**Answer:** It contracts and moves downward, increasing the volume of the chest cavity.

**Q114. [Easy]** What happens to the diaphragm during normal exhalation?

**Answer:** It relaxes and moves upward.

**Q115. [Moderate]** What is aerobic respiration?

**Answer:** Breakdown of glucose using oxygen to release energy.

**Q116. [Moderate]** What are the end products of aerobic respiration of glucose?

**Answer:** Carbon dioxide, water and energy.

**Q117. [Moderate]** What is anaerobic respiration?

**Answer:** Release of energy from glucose without using oxygen.

**Q118. [Moderate]** What are the products of anaerobic respiration in human muscles?

**Answer:** Lactic acid and energy.

**Q119. [Moderate]** What are the products of anaerobic respiration in yeast?

**Answer:** Ethanol, carbon dioxide and energy.

**Q120. [Moderate]** What is the role of cilia in the respiratory tract?

**Answer:** They help move mucus and trapped dust and microbes away from the lungs.

**Q121. [Moderate]** What is the voice box called?

**Answer:** Larynx.

**Q122. [Moderate]** What is the windpipe called?

**Answer:** Trachea.

**Q123. [Moderate]** What is the function of the nasal cavity?

**Answer:** It filters, warms and moistens inhaled air.

**Q124. [Difficult]** What is the cellular site of aerobic respiration?

**Answer:** Mainly mitochondria; glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm.

**Q125. [Difficult]** What is the chemical equation for aerobic respiration?

**Answer:** C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy.

# PART 6 – HUMAN CIRCULATORY SYSTEM

**Answer:** Aorta.

**Q134. [Easy]** Which blood vessels carry blood away from the heart?

**Q135. [Easy]** Which blood vessels carry blood towards the heart?

**Answer:** Veins.

**Q136. [Easy]** Which blood vessels connect arteries and veins?

**Answer:** Capillaries.

**Q137. [Easy]** Which blood cells transport oxygen?

**Answer:** Red blood cells.

**Q138. [Easy]** Which blood cells help fight infections?

**Answer:** White blood cells.

**Q139. [Easy]** Which blood components help in clotting?

**Answer:** Platelets.

**Q140. [Easy]** What is the liquid component of blood called?

**Answer:** Plasma.

**Q141. [Easy]** What is the red pigment in blood called?

**Answer:** Haemoglobin.

**Q142. [Easy]** Which blood group is considered the universal red-cell donor in the ABO and Rh systems?

**Answer:** O negative.

**Q143. [Easy]** Which blood group is considered the universal red-cell recipient in the ABO and Rh systems?

**Answer:** AB positive.

**Q144. [Moderate]** Which blood group is called the universal plasma donor in the ABO system?

**Answer:** AB.

**Q145. [Moderate]** Which blood group is called the universal plasma recipient in the ABO system?

**Q149. [Moderate]** What is the function of valves in the heart?

**Q152. [Moderate]** What is systemic circulation?

**Q154. [Moderate]** Which vein carries oxygenated blood?

**Q156. [Difficult]** What is the natural pacemaker of the human heart?

**Q157. [Difficult]** What is the function of the SA node?

**Answer:** It initiates electrical impulses that regulate the heartbeat.

**Q158. [Difficult]** What is the function of the septum in the human heart?

**Answer:** It separates the right and left sides of the heart and prevents mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood.

# PART 7 – EXCRETORY SYSTEM

**Q159. [Easy]** What is excretion?

**Answer:** Removal of metabolic waste products from the body.

**Q160. [Easy]** Which organs primarily remove nitrogenous wastes from human blood?

**Answer:** Kidneys.

**Q161. [Easy]** How many kidneys does a typical human have?

**Answer:** Two.

**Q162. [Easy]** What is the functional unit of the kidney?

**Answer:** Nephron.

**Q163. [Easy]** What is the main nitrogenous waste excreted by humans?

**Answer:** Urea.

**Q164. [Easy]** Where is urea produced?

**Answer:** Liver.

**Q165. [Easy]** Where is urine stored?

**Answer:** Urinary bladder.

**Q166. [Easy]** Which tube carries urine from the kidney to the urinary bladder?

**Answer:** Ureter.

**Q167. [Easy]** Which tube carries urine from the bladder out of the body?

**Answer:** Urethra.

**Q168. [Moderate]** What is glomerular filtration?

**Answer:** Filtration of blood under pressure through the glomerulus into Bowman's capsule.

**Q169. [Moderate]** What is Bowman's capsule?

**Answer:** A cup-shaped structure surrounding the glomerulus in a nephron.

**Q170. [Moderate]** What is the function of the glomerulus?

**Answer:** It filters blood to form glomerular filtrate.

**Q171. [Moderate]** What is selective reabsorption?

**Answer:** Reabsorption of useful substances and water from filtrate back into the blood.

**Q172. [Moderate]** Which hormone increases water reabsorption in the kidneys?

**Answer:** Antidiuretic hormone (ADH).

**Q173. [Moderate]** What is dialysis?

**Answer:** A procedure that removes waste products and excess fluid from blood when the kidneys cannot perform this function adequately.

**Q174. [Moderate]** What is the normal colour of urine mainly due to?

**Answer:** Urochrome pigments.

**Q175. [Difficult]** Which part of the nephron is responsible for most reabsorption of filtered nutrients and water?

**Answer:** Proximal convoluted tubule.

**Q176. [Difficult]** What is the main function of the loop of Henle?

**Answer:** It helps establish the concentration gradient in the kidney medulla, allowing urine concentration.

# PART 8 – HUMAN NERVOUS SYSTEM AND SENSE ORGANS

**Answer:** Neuron.

**Q196. [Moderate]** Which nerve carries visual information to the brain?

**Q197. [Moderate]** Which part of the eye focuses light onto the retina?

**Q198. [Moderate]** Which receptors detect smell?

**Answer:** Olfactory receptors.

**Q199. [Moderate]** Which receptors detect taste?

**Answer:** Gustatory receptors.

**Q200. [Moderate]** Which organ is responsible for hearing and balance?

**Answer:** Ear.

**Q201. [Moderate]** Which part of the ear contains the cochlea?

**Answer:** Inner ear.

**Q202. [Moderate]** What is the function of the cochlea?

**Q203. [Moderate]** Which part of the ear helps maintain body balance?

**Q204. [Difficult]** What is the function of the hypothalamus?

**Q205. [Difficult]** Which brain structure connects the two cerebral hemispheres?

# PART 10 – HUMAN REPRODUCTIVE SYSTEM

**Answer:** The biological process by which organisms produce offspring.

**Q230. [Easy]** What are the two main types of reproduction?

**Q232. [Easy]** What is asexual reproduction?

**Q233. [Easy]** What is fertilisation?

**Q234. [Easy]** What is a zygote?

**Answer:** The first cell formed after fertilisation.

**Q235. [Easy]** What is the male reproductive cell called?

**Answer:** Sperm.

**Q236. [Easy]** What is the female reproductive cell called?

**Answer:** Ovum or egg.

**Q237. [Easy]** Where are sperm produced?

**Answer:** Testes.

**Q238. [Easy]** Where are ova produced?

**Answer:** Ovaries.

**Q239. [Easy]** Where does fertilisation normally occur in humans?

**Answer:** Fallopian tube, usually in its ampullary region.

**Q240. [Easy]** Where does the embryo normally implant?

**Q255. [Easy]** Which pigment absorbs light energy during photosynthesis?

**Answer:** Xylem.

**Q271. [Moderate]** What is cross-pollination?

**Q275. [Moderate]** What does the ovule develop into after fertilisation?

**Answer:** Seed.

**Q279. [Moderate]** Which plant hormone is associated with fruit ripening?

**Answer:** Ethylene.

**Q280. [Moderate]** Which plant hormone helps in stomatal closure during water stress?

**Answer:** Abscisic acid (ABA).

**Q281. [Moderate]** What is phototropism?

**Q282. [Moderate]** What is geotropism?

**Q284. [Moderate]** What is thigmotropism?

**Q285. [Difficult]** What is the main function of root nodules in leguminous plants?

**Q311. [Moderate]** What is cartilage?

**Q318. [Difficult]** What is the main function of nervous tissue?

# PART 17 – ECOLOGY AND ENVIRONMENT

**Q438. [Easy]** What is an ecosystem?

**Answer:** A functional unit consisting of living organisms and their non-living environment interacting with one another.

**Q439. [Easy]** What are biotic components?

**Answer:** Living components of an ecosystem.

**Q440. [Easy]** What are abiotic components?

**Answer:** Non-living components such as air, water, soil, sunlight and temperature.

**Q441. [Easy]** What are producers?

**Answer:** Organisms that prepare their own food, generally through photosynthesis.

**Q442. [Easy]** What are consumers?

**Answer:** Organisms that obtain energy by feeding on other organisms.

**Q443. [Easy]** What are decomposers?

**Answer:** Organisms that break down dead organic matter and recycle nutrients.

**Q444. [Easy]** Give examples of decomposers.

**Answer:** Bacteria and fungi.

**Q445. [Easy]** What is a food chain?

**Answer:** A sequence showing the transfer of food and energy from one organism to another.

**Q446. [Easy]** What is a food web?

**Answer:** A network of interconnected food chains.

**Q447. [Easy]** What is a trophic level?

**Answer:** Each feeding level in a food chain.

**Q448. [Easy]** What is the ten per cent law of energy transfer?

**Answer:** Approximately 10% of energy at one trophic level is transferred to the next trophic level.

**Q449. [Easy]** Who proposed the ten per cent law?

**Answer:** Raymond Lindeman.

**Q450. [Moderate]** What is biological magnification?

**Answer:** Increase in the concentration of certain persistent toxic substances at higher trophic levels in a food chain.

**Q451. [Moderate]** Which chemical is a classic example of a substance that undergoes biological magnification?

**Answer:** DDT.

**Q452. [Moderate]** What is ozone?

**Answer:** A molecule consisting of three oxygen atoms, O₃.

**Q453. [Moderate]** Where is the ozone layer mainly found?

**Answer:** Stratosphere.

**Q454. [Moderate]** What is the main function of the ozone layer?

**Answer:** It absorbs much of the Sun's harmful ultraviolet radiation.

**Q455. [Moderate]** Which chemicals are historically associated with ozone depletion?

**Answer:** Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs).

**Q456. [Moderate]** What is global warming?

**Answer:** Long-term increase in Earth's average surface temperature, primarily due to increased greenhouse gases from human activities.

**Q457. [Moderate]** Name the major greenhouse gases.

**Answer:** Carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, water vapour and ozone.

**Q458. [Moderate]** What is acid rain?

**Answer:** Precipitation made unusually acidic mainly by sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides reacting in the atmosphere.

**Q459. [Moderate]** What is deforestation?

**Answer:** Large-scale removal of forest cover.

**Q460. [Moderate]** What is biodiversity?

**Answer:** Variety of life at genetic, species and ecosystem levels.

**Q461. [Moderate]** What is conservation?

**Answer:** Protection and sustainable management of natural resources and biodiversity.

**Q462. [Moderate]** What is the greenhouse effect?

**Answer:** Warming caused when greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit infrared radiation.

**Q463. [Difficult]** What is eutrophication?

**Answer:** Excessive enrichment of water with nutrients, often causing algal blooms and oxygen depletion.

**Q464. [Difficult]** What is biomagnification different from bioaccumulation?

**Answer:** Bioaccumulation is buildup of a substance within an organism over time; biomagnification is an increase in its concentration across higher trophic levels.
